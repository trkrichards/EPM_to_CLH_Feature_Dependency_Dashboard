      Terms used

      • EPM: the current platform these databases are migrating from.
      • CLH: the platform they are migrating to.
      • Database (CMX ID): one EPM site, identified by its CMX ID. The unit counted throughout.
      • ECR ID: the contract or account ID. One ECR ID can cover several databases.
      • Multi-database (Org Picker): support for organizations that run several databases.
      • SSO: single sign-on.
      • HR Download: automated user management from an HR data feed.
      • SCORM Uploader (SCO12): upload of SCORM course packages.
      • Net New, TTP Cutover, DirectCourse: account types that are not part of this EPM to CLH migration and are excluded.

      8Why we count databases, not customers

      For EPM, “customer” is a hard thing to define for a few different reasons:

      • A “customer” (one ECR) could have multiple databases if they license our content with different delivery methods. Example: Baptist Health Corbin and many others; this is common.
      • A “customer” (one or multiple ECRs) could have multiple databases if the health system level licenses some of our content, while one of the hospitals license another curriculum separately. Example: Mount Sinai
      • A “customer” (one ECR) could have multiple databases if they chose to manage the content they license on a per hospital basis, instead of at the health system level. These databases have the same ECR ID (per the contract) with an underscore a/b/c/etc., such as the VA. Work is ongoing to create unique ECR IDs that will replace the current IDs.Example: VA
      • A “customer” could consist of multiple databases that are part of a health system. This health system may or may not have multiple ECR IDs. Example: Common Spirit
      • A “customer” could consist of multiple databases loosely tied together as part of a consortium or affiliated network. This is most likely multiple ECR IDs. Example: NEAH, Medstar
      • A “customer” could potentially have more than one of the above.Example: Kaiser Permanente

      Note: The number of “customers” on EPM is not going to ever be the same as the number of “customers” on CLH, as we will need to combine EPM databases when we move them to CLH. Example: Fortis Hospitals
